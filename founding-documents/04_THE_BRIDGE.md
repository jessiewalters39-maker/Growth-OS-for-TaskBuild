# The Bridge
## How Growth OS and TaskBuild form one company
**Version 1.0 — July 2026**

*Two products, one organism. This document defines the boundary, the handoffs, the shared spine, and the loop — precisely enough that no employee on either side ever wonders whose job something is, and no customer ever gets double-texted.*

---

# 1 · The boundary, in one sentence

> **Growth OS owns strangers. TaskBuild owns relationships.**

Growth OS works the market: audiences, rankings, signals, prospect lists, past-customer *campaigns*. TaskBuild works the company: conversations, quotes, jobs, invoices, the active pipeline. The instant a stranger raises a hand — replies, calls, books, DMs — they cross the Bridge and become TaskBuild's. The instant a relationship goes dormant — job done, pipeline closed, seasons passing — its *marketing* becomes Growth OS's again, while the relationship record stays whole.

**The membrane rule:** a person is never worked by both sides at once. The Company Record marks who owns every contact at every moment, and ownership transfers are explicit, logged, baton-pass events on the Wire — never assumptions.

### The disputed territory, settled now

The blurry cases, written down so they stay sharp:

| Situation | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Open lead, live quote, active conversation | **TaskBuild** (Sales Assistant) | Active pipeline is a relationship, not a campaign. |
| Past customer, dormant, seasonal reactivation | **Growth OS** (Lifecycle) | Campaign timing on the closed book. |
| Past customer *replies* to a reactivation | **TaskBuild** (instantly) | A reply is a live conversation — cross the Bridge. |
| Review ask after a completed job | **Growth OS** (Reputation), *timed by* TaskBuild's delight signal | The ask is marketing; the timing is operations. |
| Angry review or complaint | **TaskBuild escalation path**, Reputation drafts | Complaints are relationships in danger, not content. |
| Referral lead arriving by word of mouth | **TaskBuild** (Receptionist answers), Growth OS *attributes* | Answering is operations; knowing it was a referral is analytics. |
| The owner's own social DMs that smell like leads | **TaskBuild** (Receptionist), routed by Social Manager same-minute | Speed-to-lead beats channel ownership. |
| TaskBuild's Phase-3 "Marketing Manager" and "Reputation Manager" desks | **They are Growth OS** | TaskBuild's manifesto sketched the desks; Growth OS is the building they stand in. One employee, one memory, visible in both wings. |

# 2 · The shared spine

Both products read and write **one Company Record** (TaskBuild Manifesto §04; Law 3). There is no sync, no integration, no export — architecture, not partnership. This is the moat neither product has alone:

- Growth OS knows what to *say* because TaskBuild knows what *happened* — the reactivation text references the actual 2024 gutter job; the job story cites the actual before/after; the review ask lands at the actual moment of delight.
- TaskBuild knows who's *calling* because Growth OS knows who was *courted* — the Receptionist answers the reactivated customer already knowing which campaign warmed them and which roof they own.
- Attribution is a birthright, not a project: source at birth, revenue at close, one ledger.

# 3 · The relays

*In the tradition of the TaskBuild Manifesto's "life of a job" — the Bridge, shown as baton passes. Every pass is a logged, attributed event on the Wire.*

### Relay one — the life of a stranger

1. **Market Researcher** (Growth OS) — finds the prospect: right trade-adjacency, right zip, a real signal. Enriches, scores Hot, writes the *why*.
2. **Outbound SDR** (Growth OS) — three touches over two weeks, each citing something true. Touch three lands.
3. *The reply.* **Crossing event logged.** The SDR hands over everything: research, touches, the reply, the read on intent.
4. **Receptionist** (TaskBuild) — responds in minutes, warm and briefed. Qualifies. Books Thursday 9 AM.
5. **Estimator → Dispatcher → the job** (TaskBuild) — operations does what operations does.
6. **Customer Success** (TaskBuild) — next-day check-in: thrilled.
7. **Reputation Manager** (Growth OS) — reads "thrilled" → review ask at the peak. Five stars, responded to in an hour.
8. **Content Writer** (Growth OS) — the job becomes a story; the story becomes a page; **Local SEO** watches the page start ranking for the neighborhood.
9. **Lifecycle Marketer** (Growth OS) — sets the clock: service anniversary, season, system age. The stranger is now an asset that compounds.
10. **Growth Analyst** — books the whole thread: this signal type, this sequence, this zip = one job at known cost. *Next month's plan just got smarter.*

### Relay two — the life of a quiet week

1. **Dispatcher** (TaskBuild) — next week's board is 40% empty. The capacity signal posts to the Record.
2. **Growth Director** (Growth OS) — reads it same-day. Surge plan drafted: 20 reactivation drafts (gutter season, customers 18+ months quiet), LSA budget +$300 for ten days, one neighborhood post. **One approval requested.**
3. **Owner** — one tap over coffee.
4. **Lifecycle + Ads Manager** (Growth OS) — execute within the approved envelope.
5. **Receptionist** (TaskBuild) — answers what comes back; books six jobs into the empty board.
6. **Growth Analyst** — the surge cost $412 and filled $9,300 of schedule. It's in Friday's briefing, as money.

### Relay three — the life of a referral

1. **Customer Success** (TaskBuild) — 30-day check-in on the Hendersons: still thrilled.
2. **Lifecycle Marketer** (Growth OS) — the referral ask, drafted in the owner's voice, at the right moment: *"know anyone on the street dealing with the same ice-dam mess?"*
3. The neighbor calls. **Receptionist** answers; **Analyst** tags the source: referral, Henderson thread, $0 acquisition cost.
4. **Growth Director** — referral rate ticks up in the weekly review; recommends the neighborhood post Content drafted anyway. The street becomes a cluster of five customers by spring.

# 4 · The Demand Thermostat

The closed loop enables the one capability no agency, tool, or standalone product can offer, because none of them can see the schedule board:

> **Demand generation throttled by operational capacity, automatically.**

- **Calendar thin next week** → surge: reactivation batches, LSA budget up, seasonal push out — *proposed same-day, owner-approved, executed in hours.*
- **Crews buried three weeks out** → ease: paid spend down within approved bounds, outbound paced, demand banked instead of wasted — *with a note explaining the money it didn't spend.*
- **The seasonal ramp** → the thermostat learns the business's rhythm and starts campaigns *before* the season, not during the panic (elite teams' "seasonal readiness" — automated).

The thermostat is the Bridge's signature feature and the ecosystem's sharpest sales argument: **marketing that knows when to stop is marketing an owner can finally trust.** No agency ever eased its own billable spend because the client was too busy. Growth OS does it as a design principle.

# 5 · SLAs on the Bridge

Handoffs without deadlines are where revenue leaks (Research Foundation, Part II). The Bridge's SLAs are written in the Record and reported by the Growth Analyst — both directions:

| Handoff | SLA | Watched by |
|---|---|---|
| Warm reply (Growth OS) → first response (TaskBuild) | **Minutes.** Speed-to-lead is a 21x lever; it's the whole point of having both halves. | Growth Analyst |
| Hot signal (Researcher) → first touch drafted (SDR) | 4 hours | Growth Director |
| Job completed + happy (TaskBuild) → review ask (Reputation) | Same day — the emotional peak has a half-life | Reputation Manager |
| Capacity signal (Dispatcher) → thermostat proposal (Growth Director) | Same day | Growth Director |
| Ownership transfer of any contact | Logged before the next touch, always | The Record itself — untransferred double-touches are bugs, filed as such |

# 6 · One company, two subscriptions, honest alone

**Together is the design center.** The demo, the pricing page, the onboarding all assume the pair: *TaskBuild runs your company. Growth OS grows it.* The bundle is the story of hiring a whole staff.

**But each half must be honest alone.**
- *TaskBuild without Growth OS* is complete — it already was. It answers, quotes, dispatches, follows up on its own pipeline. (It simply grows at the speed of its own inbound.)
- *Growth OS without TaskBuild* must genuinely work: leads land in a simple pipeline, handoffs go to the owner's phone instead of a Receptionist, completion signals come from a one-tap "job done?" instead of a Dispatcher. Degraded, not crippled — an upsell wearing a mission is a violation of the Decision Filter's question 6, and customers smell it.
- *The upgrade moment is organic:* the day Growth OS makes the phone ring more than the owner can answer is the day TaskBuild sells itself. And vice versa: the day TaskBuild's Wire shows a quiet week is the day the owner asks what a growth department would cost.

**Customer zero closes the loop.** Growth OS v0 grows TaskBuild itself; TaskBuild's customers become Growth OS's Phase-1 market; every play ships to customers only after it worked on us. The two products don't just integrate — they raised each other.
